# Role, authorization and evidence

This matrix does not decide whether somebody is a “real expert”. It helps establish the capacity in which a particular claim is made.

| Role | Typical access | Source of authorization | Verifiable output |
|---|---|---|---|
| Red Team operator | In-scope systems, test infrastructure, sometimes physical or social-engineering scenarios | Contract and rules of engagement | Evidence of objectives, detection gaps, report |
| Penetration tester | Defined applications, networks, or controls | Contract and technical scope | Reproducible findings, risk assessment, recommendations |
| Blue Team / SOC analyst | Telemetry, alerts, logs, defensive tools | Job responsibilities and organisational procedures | Detection, triage, escalation, defensive improvements |
| Incident responder | Logs, network flows, endpoint data, incident timeline | Incident-management mandate | Containment, eradication, recovery, lessons learned |
| Digital forensics investigator | Disk and memory images, artefacts, chain of custody | Investigative mandate and procedures | Methodically acquired and analysed evidence |
| CTI analyst | Internal and external sources, observations, reports | Organisational intelligence requirement | Source- and confidence-based assessment |
| Security researcher | Laboratory, public sources, policy-authorised assets | Ownership, contract, or programme policy | Reproducible research or vulnerability report |
| Journalist | Sources, interviews, documents, public data | Editorial assignment and source-protection principles | A verified, public-interest story |
| External commentator | Primarily public statements and secondary sources | No operational mandate unless explicitly stated | Explanation that discloses sources and uncertainty |

## The right question

Not: “Does this person sound like a cybersecurity expert?”

Instead:

> What was their role, what were they allowed to do, what could they access, and what evidence supports the specific claim?

One person may hold several roles at different times. What matters is the role in which they are speaking or acting in the particular situation.
