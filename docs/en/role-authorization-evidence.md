# Role, authorization and evidence

This matrix does not decide whether somebody is a “real expert”. It helps establish the capacity in which a particular claim is made.

| Role | Typical access | Operational mandate | System/data authorization | Legal or policy constraints | Verifiable output |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Red Team operator | In-scope systems, test infrastructure, sometimes physical or social-engineering scenarios | Agreed assessment objectives | Contract, asset-owner approval, and rules of engagement | Time, methods, safety stops, third parties, data handling | Evidence of objectives, detection gaps, report |
| Penetration tester | Defined applications, networks, or controls | Commissioned assessment | Contract and technical scope | Client authority, supplier terms, privacy, data handling | Reproducible findings, risk assessment, recommendations |
| Blue Team / SOC analyst | Telemetry, alerts, logs, defensive tools | Defensive duties and procedures | Role-based organisational access | Monitoring purpose, retention, privacy, escalation rules | Detection, triage, escalation, defensive improvements |
| Incident responder | Logs, network flows, endpoint data, incident timeline | Incident-management mandate | Incident-specific or standing organisational access | Evidence preservation, privacy, regulator and law-enforcement duties | Containment, eradication, recovery, lessons learned |
| Digital forensics investigator | Disk and memory images, artefacts, chain of custody | Investigative instruction | Explicit evidence-acquisition authority | Jurisdiction, proportionality, chain of custody, privilege | Methodically acquired and analysed evidence |
| CTI analyst | Internal and external sources, observations, reports | Intelligence requirement | Approved source and platform access | Collection restrictions, source protection, confidence rules | Source- and confidence-based assessment |
| Security researcher | Laboratory, public sources, policy-authorised assets | Research objective | Ownership, contract, or explicit programme policy | Scope, methods, privacy, third-party assets, disclosure terms | Reproducible research or vulnerability report |
| Journalist | Sources, interviews, documents, public data | Editorial assignment | No implied system access; access depends on lawful source and method | Media law, privacy, source protection, public-interest assessment | A verified, public-interest story |
| External commentator | Primarily public statements and secondary sources | None unless separately stated | No implied operational access | Accuracy, attribution, confidentiality, investigation sensitivity | Explanation that discloses sources and uncertainty |

## The right question

Not: “Does this person sound like a cybersecurity expert?”

Instead:

> What was their role, what were they allowed to do, what could they access, and what evidence supports the specific claim?

One person may hold several roles at different times. What matters is the role in which they are speaking or acting in the particular situation.
