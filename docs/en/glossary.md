# Complete cybersecurity glossary

This page renders every canonical term from the machine-readable dataset. Status labels describe how the term is used; they do not rank people or grant permission.

## Actors and identity labels

<a id="term-hacker"></a>
### Hacker

<span class="term-status">established_with_context</span>

A broad cultural or technical label whose meaning ranges from a curious, skilled system explorer to an unauthorised user, depending on the source and context.

**Authorization boundary:** The label alone establishes no permission to test a system.

**Sources:** [Internet Users' Glossary — RFC 1392](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc1392.html), [Hacker — CSRC Glossary](https://csrc.nist.gov/glossary/term/hacker), [Hacker — The Jargon File](https://www.catb.org/jargon/html/H/hacker.html), [Hacker Ethics](https://www.ccc.de/en/hackerethik)

<a id="term-cracker"></a>
### Cracker

<span class="term-status">historical_or_community_jargon</span>

A historical community term for someone who defeats security controls, sometimes also used for software-protection circumvention.

**Authorization boundary:** The term does not determine authorization; describe the actual conduct.

**Sources:** [Internet Users' Glossary — RFC 1392](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc1392.html), [Cracker — The Jargon File](https://www.catb.org/jargon/html/C/cracker.html)

<a id="term-attacker"></a>
### Attacker

<span class="term-status">established</span>

A person attempting to exploit vulnerabilities or otherwise compromise a system's security.

**Authorization boundary:** In ordinary use, attack activity is adversarial; authorised simulation should be stated explicitly.

**Sources:** [Attacker — CSRC Glossary](https://csrc.nist.gov/glossary/term/attacker)

<a id="term-threat_actor"></a>
### Threat actor

<span class="term-status">established</span>

An individual or group associated with activity that presents a cybersecurity threat.

**Authorization boundary:** Attribution to a named actor requires evidence and a stated confidence level.

**Sources:** [Threat Actor — CSRC Glossary](https://csrc.nist.gov/glossary/term/threat_actor)

<a id="term-hacktivist"></a>
### Hacktivist

<span class="term-status">established_with_context</span>

An actor who associates cyber activity with a political or social cause.

**Authorization boundary:** Motivation does not establish legality, attribution, truthfulness, or proportionality.

**Sources:** [ENISA Threat Landscape 2025](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/publications/enisa-threat-landscape-2025)

## Professional roles

<a id="term-security_researcher"></a>
### Security researcher

<span class="term-status">established_with_context</span>

A context-sensitive label for a person who researches cybersecurity or investigates vulnerabilities; formal research roles and vulnerability-finder roles may have different mandates.

**Authorization boundary:** The role title does not itself authorize testing a particular third-party system.

**Sources:** [European Cybersecurity Skills Framework — Role Profiles](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/publications/european-cybersecurity-skills-framework-role-profiles), [CERT Guide to Coordinated Vulnerability Disclosure](https://certcc.github.io/CERT-Guide-to-CVD/tutorials/terms/cvd/), [Hacker Ethics](https://www.ccc.de/en/hackerethik)

<a id="term-ethical_hacker"></a>
### Ethical hacker

<span class="term-status">established_with_context</span>

A widely recognised but context-sensitive label; ENISA lists Ethical Hacker as an alternative title under the Penetration Tester profile, while community usage may be broader.

**Authorization boundary:** Ethical intent is not a substitute for scope, rules, and legal authorization.

**Sources:** [European Cybersecurity Skills Framework — Role Profiles](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/publications/european-cybersecurity-skills-framework-role-profiles)

<a id="term-penetration_tester"></a>
### Penetration tester

<span class="term-status">established</span>

A practitioner who performs a planned security assessment within a defined technical scope and rules of engagement.

**Authorization boundary:** Authorization normally derives from a contract, statement of work, and rules of engagement.

**Sources:** [European Cybersecurity Skills Framework — Role Profiles](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/publications/european-cybersecurity-skills-framework-role-profiles), [Rules of Engagement — CSRC Glossary](https://csrc.nist.gov/glossary/term/rules_of_engagement), [Red Teaming](https://www.crest-approved.org/cyber-service-categories/red-teaming/), [Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/1190 on threat-led penetration testing](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32025R1190)

<a id="term-incident_responder"></a>
### Cyber incident responder

<span class="term-status">established</span>

A practitioner who analyses, contains, eradicates, and supports recovery from cybersecurity incidents.

**Authorization boundary:** Access and actions derive from the organisation's incident-management mandate and procedures.

**Sources:** [European Cybersecurity Skills Framework — Role Profiles](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/publications/european-cybersecurity-skills-framework-role-profiles), [CSIRT Services Framework v2.1](https://www.first.org/standards/frameworks/csirts/csirt_services_framework_v2.1)

<a id="term-digital_forensics_investigator"></a>
### Digital forensics investigator

<span class="term-status">established</span>

A practitioner who acquires, preserves, analyses, and reports digital evidence using documented methods.

**Authorization boundary:** The mandate, evidence handling, and legal context determine permitted actions.

**Sources:** [European Cybersecurity Skills Framework — Role Profiles](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/publications/european-cybersecurity-skills-framework-role-profiles)

<a id="term-cyber_threat_intelligence_specialist"></a>
### Cyber threat intelligence specialist

<span class="term-status">established</span>

A practitioner who collects, evaluates, and communicates information about cyber threats with stated sourcing and confidence.

**Authorization boundary:** Intelligence assessment does not itself authorise interaction with an adversary's infrastructure.

**Sources:** [European Cybersecurity Skills Framework — Role Profiles](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/publications/european-cybersecurity-skills-framework-role-profiles)

## Team functions

<a id="term-red_team"></a>
### Red Team

<span class="term-status">established</span>

An authorised group that emulates adversary capabilities to assess an organisation's prevention, detection, and response.

**Authorization boundary:** The activity is bounded by objectives, scope, and rules of engagement.

**Sources:** [Red Team — CSRC Glossary](https://csrc.nist.gov/glossary/term/red_team), [Rules of Engagement — CSRC Glossary](https://csrc.nist.gov/glossary/term/rules_of_engagement), [Adversary Emulation and Red Teaming](https://attack.mitre.org/resources/get-started/adversary-emulation-and-red-teaming/), [Red Teaming](https://www.crest-approved.org/cyber-service-categories/red-teaming/), [Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/1190 on threat-led penetration testing](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32025R1190)

<a id="term-blue_team"></a>
### Blue Team

<span class="term-status">established_with_context</span>

The defensive side in an exercise or assessment; broader operational usage may include monitoring, detection, analysis, response, and other defensive tasks.

**Authorization boundary:** Exact responsibilities vary between organisations and may overlap SOC, CSIRT, IR, and engineering.

**Sources:** [Blue Team — CSRC Glossary](https://csrc.nist.gov/glossary/term/blue_team), [Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/1190 on threat-led penetration testing](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32025R1190)

<a id="term-purple_teaming"></a>
### Purple teaming

<span class="term-status">established_with_context</span>

A collaborative practice in which offensive techniques and defensive observations are combined to improve detection and response.

**Authorization boundary:** It may be a mode of work or a formal team; neither form expands the authorised test scope.

**Sources:** [Adversary Emulation and Red Teaming](https://attack.mitre.org/resources/get-started/adversary-emulation-and-red-teaming/), [Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/1190 on threat-led penetration testing](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32025R1190)

<a id="term-white_team"></a>
### White Team

<span class="term-status">established</span>

The exercise-control function that enforces rules, coordinates safety, observes activity, and judges outcomes.

**Authorization boundary:** The White Team governs an exercise; it is not the same as a white-hat hacker.

**Sources:** [White Team — CSRC Glossary](https://csrc.nist.gov/glossary/term/white_team)

## Organisational functions

<a id="term-soc"></a>
### Security Operations Centre (SOC)

<span class="term-status">established_with_context</span>

A capability whose core is continuous monitoring, detection, and event analysis; triage, escalation, and response responsibilities vary by mandate.

**Authorization boundary:** SOC staff act under organisational responsibilities and access controls.

**Sources:** [Team Types Within the Context of Services Frameworks — Version 1.2](https://www.first.org/standards/frameworks/csirts/team-type_1.2)

<a id="term-csirt"></a>
### Computer Security Incident Response Team (CSIRT)

<span class="term-status">established</span>

A team that provides services for receiving, analysing, coordinating, mitigating, and recovering from security incidents for a defined constituency.

**Authorization boundary:** Authority and constituency are defined by the team's mandate.

**Sources:** [Team Types Within the Context of Services Frameworks — Version 1.2](https://www.first.org/standards/frameworks/csirts/team-type_1.2), [CSIRT Services Framework v2.1](https://www.first.org/standards/frameworks/csirts/csirt_services_framework_v2.1)

<a id="term-psirt"></a>
### Product Security Incident Response Team (PSIRT)

<span class="term-status">established</span>

A team that receives and assesses product-vulnerability reports and coordinates disposition, remediation, and disclosure with the responsible product functions.

**Authorization boundary:** Its mandate concerns the organisation's products and coordinated product-security process.

**Sources:** [Team Types Within the Context of Services Frameworks — Version 1.2](https://www.first.org/standards/frameworks/csirts/team-type_1.2), [PSIRT Services Framework v1.1](https://www.first.org/standards/frameworks/psirts/psirt_services_framework_v1.1)

## Authorization

<a id="term-authorised_testing"></a>
### Authorised security testing

<span class="term-status">established_with_context</span>

Security testing performed under permission that defines the target, time, methods, constraints, reporting route, and responsible parties.

**Authorization boundary:** Authorization is bounded; activity outside the stated scope is not covered automatically.

**Sources:** [Rules of Engagement — CSRC Glossary](https://csrc.nist.gov/glossary/term/rules_of_engagement), [Gold Standard Safe Harbor Statement](https://docs.hackerone.com/en/articles/8494525-gold-standard-safe-harbor-statement)

## Vulnerability processes

<a id="term-cvd"></a>
### Coordinated Vulnerability Disclosure (CVD)

<span class="term-status">established</span>

A process for receiving, analysing, coordinating, remediating or mitigating, and disclosing vulnerability information among relevant parties.

**Authorization boundary:** CVD is not universal permission to test; a specific programme may define authorised in-scope research.

**Sources:** [CERT Guide to Coordinated Vulnerability Disclosure](https://certcc.github.io/CERT-Guide-to-CVD/tutorials/terms/cvd/), [Guidelines and Practices for Multi-Party Vulnerability Coordination and Disclosure](https://www.first.org/global/sigs/vulnerability-coordination/multiparty/guidelines-v1.1), [PSIRT Services Framework v1.1](https://www.first.org/standards/frameworks/psirts/psirt_services_framework_v1.1), [Koordinētas ievainojamību atklāšanas platformas BUJ](https://cvd.cert.lv/faq/answers/100), [ISO/IEC 29147:2018 Vulnerability disclosure](https://www.iso.org/standard/72311.html)

<a id="term-vulnerability_disclosure_policy"></a>
### Vulnerability Disclosure Policy (VDP)

<span class="term-status">established</span>

An organisation's public policy describing reporting channels, scope, expected researcher conduct, handling, and often safe-harbour commitments.

**Authorization boundary:** The applicable policy may state the issuer's offered permission and safe-harbour boundaries; their effect also depends on the issuer's authority, third-party rights, contracts, and applicable law.

**Sources:** [BOD 20-01 — Develop and Publish a Vulnerability Disclosure Policy](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/directives/bod-20-01-develop-and-publish-vulnerability-disclosure-policy), [A File Format to Aid in Security Vulnerability Disclosure — RFC 9116](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9116.html)

<a id="term-vulnerability_disclosure_program"></a>
### Vulnerability Disclosure Program (VDP)

<span class="term-status">established_with_context</span>

An organisational capability for receiving, triaging, coordinating, remediating, and communicating vulnerability reports; its policy is one component of the programme.

**Authorization boundary:** A programme may publish permission or a safe-harbour commitment; the applicable policy states the offered boundaries, subject to the issuer's authority, third-party rights, contracts, and applicable law.

**Sources:** [CERT Guide to Coordinated Vulnerability Disclosure](https://certcc.github.io/CERT-Guide-to-CVD/tutorials/terms/cvd/), [Establishing a Coordinated Vulnerability Disclosure Program](https://www.cisa.gov/resources-tools/resources/establishing-coordinated-vulnerability-disclosure-program-work-security-researchers)

<a id="term-security_txt"></a>
### security.txt

<span class="term-status">established</span>

An IETF-defined, machine-parsable text format that helps researchers locate an organisation's security contact and disclosure-policy information.

**Authorization boundary:** The presence or absence of the file does not grant or revoke testing permission.

**Sources:** [A File Format to Aid in Security Vulnerability Disclosure — RFC 9116](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9116.html)

<a id="term-bug_bounty"></a>
### Bug Bounty programme

<span class="term-status">established</span>

A programme that invites policy-compliant vulnerability reports and offers stated monetary or non-monetary rewards for qualifying findings; not every submission qualifies for payment.

**Authorization boundary:** Authorization and reward eligibility are bounded by programme scope and rules.

**Sources:** [BOD 20-01 — Develop and Publish a Vulnerability Disclosure Policy](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/directives/bod-20-01-develop-and-publish-vulnerability-disclosure-policy), [Koordinētas ievainojamību atklāšanas platformas BUJ](https://cvd.cert.lv/faq/answers/100), [Gold Standard Safe Harbor Statement](https://docs.hackerone.com/en/articles/8494525-gold-standard-safe-harbor-statement)

<a id="term-responsible_disclosure"></a>
### Responsible disclosure

<span class="term-status">common_but_contested</span>

A historical and still-used label for vulnerability reporting that often implies private notice before publication.

**Authorization boundary:** The label does not define testing permission, timing, or a complete coordination process.

**Sources:** [CERT Guide to Coordinated Vulnerability Disclosure](https://certcc.github.io/CERT-Guide-to-CVD/tutorials/terms/cvd/), [Guidelines and Practices for Multi-Party Vulnerability Coordination and Disclosure](https://www.first.org/global/sigs/vulnerability-coordination/multiparty/guidelines-v1.1)

<a id="term-full_disclosure"></a>
### Full disclosure

<span class="term-status">established_with_context</span>

A disclosure approach involving public release of vulnerability detail before remediation is available; the amount and timing of published detail vary.

**Authorization boundary:** The term does not establish prior authorisation to discover or test the vulnerability.

**Sources:** [Guidelines and Practices for Multi-Party Vulnerability Coordination and Disclosure](https://www.first.org/global/sigs/vulnerability-coordination/multiparty/guidelines-v1.1), [RFPolicy 2.0](https://dl.packetstormsecurity.net/papers/general/rfpolicy-2.0.txt)

## Community jargon

<a id="term-white_hat_hacker"></a>
### White-hat hacker

<span class="term-status">established_with_context</span>

An informal label for someone conducting legitimate, authorised defensive security testing.

**Authorization boundary:** The colour label is insufficient; the concrete scope and rules still control.

**Sources:** [White hat — Cyber.gov.au glossary](https://www.cyber.gov.au/glossary/white-hat)

<a id="term-script_kiddie"></a>
### Script kiddie / skid

<span class="term-status">historical_or_community_jargon</span>

A pejorative community label for someone perceived to use ready-made tools without sufficient understanding.

**Authorization boundary:** Skill level does not determine whether activity is authorised.

**Sources:** [Script Kiddies: What Are They and What Are They Doing?](https://www.giac.org/paper/gsec/244/script-kiddies-what-doing/100752)

## Communication roles

<a id="term-public_commentator"></a>
### Cyber incident commentator

<span class="term-status">project_descriptive_category</span>

A descriptive communication role for a person who publicly explains cyber incidents, often using public or secondary information.

**Authorization boundary:** It is not a standard operational role and does not imply access to investigative evidence.

**Sources:** [Effective communications in a cyber incident](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/guidance/effective-communications-in-a-cyber-incident)
